postlink Analysis Report

August 30, 2026

Data

The linked dataset contains 156,453 records. The outcome variable is age at death (years) and the main predictor is year of birth.

Methods

Linkage error was accounted for using postlink (R package, v0.1.0; Bukke et al., 2026). The outcome (age at death (years)) was modelled using Gaussian (linear) regression with formula age_at_death ~ poly(unit_yob, 3, raw = TRUE). Linkage error correction used an EM-based two-component mixture model (Slawski et al., 2025). Each record pair is modelled as either a correct match (C = 1) or a false link (C = 0). The EM algorithm alternates between estimating posterior correct match probabilities (E-step) and updating the regression parameters for true matches (M-step). The EM algorithm converged. The mean posterior correct match probability was 0.9 (65.6% of records exceeded 0.9), implying an estimated mismatch rate of approximately 10%.

Results

The narrative below was drafted by an AI language model using exact numerical outputs from the model. Review and edit before submitting or publishing.
The EM mixture model–adjusted estimates, which correct for the approximately 5% expected mismatch rate among machine-linked records, reveal a statistically significant nonlinear relationship between year of birth and age at death across all three polynomial terms (all p < 0.05). The adjusted intercept was 58.63 (95% CI: 58.34, 58.91), with linear, quadratic, and cubic year-of-birth coefficients of −51.17 (95% CI: −54.20, −48.13), 140.54 (95% CI: 132.95, 148.12), and −76.92 (95% CI: −82.01, −71.83), respectively. Compared to the naive model estimated on all records without linkage correction, the adjusted linear term was meaningfully more negative (−51.17 vs. −46.70), the quadratic term was larger (140.54 vs. 130.41), and the cubic term was more strongly negative (−76.92 vs. −72.94), suggesting that false links in the naive model attenuated the estimated curvature of the birth-year–longevity relationship. The adjusted estimates also diverged modestly from those obtained using the small hand-linked subsample alone (n = 2,159), with the full adjusted model yielding more pronounced polynomial coefficients, reflecting greater statistical precision while still accounting for linkage error.
